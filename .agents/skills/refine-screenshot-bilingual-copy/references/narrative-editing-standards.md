# Portfolio Narrative Editing Standards

Use these standards after mapping the screenshot to source. Apply judgment by block function; do not turn the page into a repeated template.

## 1. Editorial role

Edit with the combined judgment expected of:

- AI Product Designer
- UX Researcher
- Product Designer
- Data Product / Product Analyst
- Technical PM
- Front-End Product Engineer

Show only the capabilities relevant to the selected project and block. Do not force every discipline into one paragraph.

## 2. Block functions

Determine the job of each selected block before rewriting:

| Block | Primary editorial question |
|---|---|
| Project positioning | What is this project, for whom, and why does it matter? |
| Problem context | Who encountered what problem, and why was the existing approach insufficient? |
| Usage scenario | What was the real workflow, environment, or constraint? |
| Research method | What was examined, with whom, how, and with what limitation? |
| Research insight | What pattern was observed, and what evidence supports it? |
| Product strategy | What was prioritized or excluded, and why? |
| Design decision | What choice was made, what alternatives or constraints shaped it, and what changed? |
| Technical architecture | How does the system work, and which engineering tradeoff matters? |
| Validation evidence | What was measured or observed, and how strong is the evidence? |
| Shipped output | What was actually delivered, by whom, and in what product state? |
| Reflection / limitation | What remains uncertain, risky, incomplete, or worth changing next? |

## 3. Truth and evidence

- Never invent users, growth, conversion, adoption, revenue, research results, performance, dates, awards, product status, or deployment state.
- Never upgrade a concept, mock, prototype, reconstruction, or simulation into a shipped product.
- Never present team output as solely individual work.
- Preserve authenticity labels such as real, reconstructed, simulated, illustrative, prototype, pilot, MVP, and production.
- Describe research observations as observations. Do not imply causation without causal evidence.
- Keep sample sizes, methods, time windows, denominators, units, and caveats attached to the relevant claim.
- For AI products, preserve model limitations, human review, source traceability, failure modes, safety boundaries, and escalation paths.
- When evidence is directional rather than conclusive, say so naturally instead of strengthening the claim.
- If a fact is uncertain, retain the safest supported wording and list the uncertainty in the final report.

## 4. Taiwanese Traditional Chinese voice

Write like a practitioner explaining work they know well, not like brand advertising or an AI summary.

Prefer:

- an explicit subject when agency matters;
- active voice;
- concrete verbs;
- one core idea per sentence;
- specific nouns instead of abstract containers;
- natural transitions between problem, decision, action, and evidence;
- concise, human phrasing used in Taiwan.

Reduce or replace vague, promotional, or formulaic wording such as:

- 打造
- 賦能
- 旨在
- 致力於
- 多維度
- 全方位
- 深度整合
- 無縫體驗
- 高效能
- 智慧化
- 端到端
- 完整閉環
- 不僅……更……
- 透過……進行……
- 可以被捍衛
- 產品賭注
- unsupported metaphors such as 訊號、節奏、駕駛艙

These terms are not absolutely banned. Keep one only when it is concrete, accurate, and natural in context.

Avoid:

- direct English syntax carried into Chinese;
- repeated sentence openings;
- stacked nominal phrases with no clear actor;
- inflated claims such as「大幅提升」「成功解決」without evidence;
- vague references such as「此方案」「這部分」「相關內容」when a precise noun is available;
- turning every paragraph into「問題—解法—成果」regardless of its role.

## 5. Portfolio narrative logic

Use the links that help the selected block:

`Context → Problem → Decision → Action → Evidence → Outcome → Limitation`

Each block should answer one to three of:

- Who encountered what problem?
- Why was the existing approach insufficient?
- What judgment did I or the team make?
- Why was this option selected?
- What action followed?
- What evidence supports the decision?
- What changed as a result?
- What remains limited or uncertain?

Do not require every block to contain the full chain. Across a page, vary the narrative function and avoid repeating the same setup.

## 6. Length and hierarchy

Match the component and screenshot. Clarity does not justify visible overflow.

| UI element | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Section label | 2–8 Chinese characters when practical |
| Main heading | One clear point; avoid combining more than two concepts |
| Supporting copy | Usually 2–4 sentences |
| Summary card | Usually 1–2 sentences |
| Button / CTA | State what appears or happens after activation |
| Metric label | State the unit and what the number represents |
| Technical label | Follow the page's existing Title Case convention |

- Do not repeat the heading in the first sentence.
- Preserve deliberate line breaks, list counts, and information hierarchy.
- Prefer cutting repetition over shrinking meaning.
- If revised copy still risks overflow, report the risk instead of changing CSS or layout.

## 7. Role-relevant evidence

Surface the most relevant capabilities through the work itself:

- problem definition and scope management;
- research evidence translated into decisions;
- AI capability boundaries and human intervention;
- metrics, risk, and validation design;
- data governance, provenance, and traceability;
- cross-functional collaboration and delivery;
- component states, accessibility, and engineering tradeoffs.

Do not list job titles or competencies as claims when the project narrative can demonstrate them.

## 8. Page-wide consistency checks

Even when the edit scope is narrow, check the full page for consistency with the revised block:

- project name and product state;
- terminology and bilingual equivalents;
- numbers, units, sample sizes, and dates;
- module names, navigation labels, page titles, and repeated metadata;
- prototype, simulation, reconstruction, pilot, MVP, and production distinctions;
- English capitalization and technical terminology;
- individual versus team attribution;
- duplicated facts or claims in adjacent sections.

Only edit out-of-scope occurrences when they are the same shared label or metadata required to keep the targeted concept consistent. Otherwise report the inconsistency without expanding scope.

## 9. English revision

- Write natural portfolio English, not a literal translation.
- Preserve claim strength and uncertainty.
- Keep the same subject, attribution, evidence, product state, metrics, and limitations.
- Prefer precise verbs and compact sentences.
- Avoid generic product language such as empower, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, holistic, end-to-end, or transformative unless specifically supported.
- Match established product names, role names, capitalization, and terminology elsewhere on the page.
- Keep parallel arrays aligned by meaning and order.
